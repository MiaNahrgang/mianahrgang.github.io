I am a Postdoctoral Researcher at the University of Konstanz, where I am part of the [Communication, Networks and Contention Group](https://www.polver.uni-konstanz.de/cnc/) led by Nils Weidmann. My dissertation, *The Politics of Content Moderation: Policies, Pressure, and Participation*, examines how social media platforms govern online speech across three empirical studies.

The first chapter introduces COMPARE, a new dataset covering 132 social media platforms, and analyzes the complexity of 89 community guidelines along dimensions of length, readability, and semantic complexity — finding that larger platforms tend to have the longest and most complex guidelines, and that guidelines have grown longer in response to new regulation. The second chapter investigates how platforms respond to political pressure during high-stakes moments such as elections, drawing on 13.4 million moderation decisions by Bluesky across four countries; it finds that platforms moderate content faster during election periods without sacrificing accuracy. The third chapter evaluates public participation in platform governance by analyzing nearly 4,000 public comments submitted to the Meta Oversight Board, finding that stakeholder input has both a quantitative and qualitative influence on the Board's decisions — suggesting that the Oversight Board is a meaningful mechanism for democratizing platform governance, not merely symbolic.

My research sits within the project Transparency in Content Moderation, led by Margaret Roberts (UC San Diego), Nils Weidmann (Konstanz), and Yannis Theocharis (TU Munich). To study these questions, I draw on computational methods, including large-scale text analysis and natural language processing.
